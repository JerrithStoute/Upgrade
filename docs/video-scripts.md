# The Builder Station — instructional video scripts

## How to use these

- **One video = one job to get done.** Keep each one 2–5 minutes. Short videos are easier to re-record when a screen changes.
- **Record in the sample job** (Sample Home, 123 Sample Lane) or Whitfield, never a real client's job. Nothing about the sample job is ever emailed or sent to QuickBooks.
- **Each script has two columns of instructions:**
  - **DO** — what to click on screen.
  - **SAY** — what to say while you do it. Say it in your own words; it doesn't need to be word for word.
- **Before recording:** close other tabs, turn off notifications, zoom the browser to 110–125% so text is readable, and move the mouse slowly.
- **If you make a mistake,** pause two seconds and redo the line. You can cut it out later.

## Video 1 — A tour: how the program fits together

**Goal:** a new builder knows what each part of the program is for, and the order work flows through it.
**Start on:** the Dashboard, signed in as an admin.

| | DO | SAY |
|---|----|-----|
| 1 | Show the Dashboard. | "This is The Builder Station. It runs a building job start to finish in one place — from the plans to the last invoice. Let me show you how the pieces fit together." |
| 2 | Move the mouse slowly down the menu on the left. | "Down the left is the menu: Leads, Projects, Clients, the Schedule, Invoices, Bills, Reports, and Settings. You only see the parts your position allows." |
| 3 | Click **Projects**, then open **Sample Home**. | "Every job lives under Projects. I'll open the sample job — it's a made-up house with everything filled in, so you can click around without hurting anything." |
| 4 | Point at the tabs across the top of the job. | "Inside a job, these tabs follow the job from start to finish. Here's the order work flows." |
| 5 | Click **Plans**. | "First, the plans come in. You drop the PDFs here." |
| 6 | Click **Takeoff**, open a sheet. | "Then you measure them — that's the takeoff. Every wall, floor and roof you measure is priced from your own price list as you go." |
| 7 | Click **Estimate**. | "The takeoff fills in the estimate by itself. You add the rest — subs, allowances, your markup." |
| 8 | Click **View proposal**. | "The estimate becomes the proposal — what the client sees. Your costs and markup never show on it." |
| 9 | Go back, click **Budget**. | "Once the client signs, the estimate becomes the job's budget. Bills land against it, so you always know where you stand." |
| 10 | Click **Purchasing**, then **Invoices**. | "From there you order with purchase orders, and bill the client with invoices." |
| 11 | Click **Schedule**, **Selections**, **Change Orders**, **Daily Logs**. | "And you run the job with the schedule, the client's selections, change orders and daily logs." |
| 12 | Click the **?** at the top of the page. | "Anywhere you get stuck, click the question mark. It opens the manual right to the page you're on." |
| 13 | Back to the Dashboard. | "That's the whole loop: plans, takeoff, estimate, proposal, budget, and running the job. Each of the next videos covers one of those pieces." |

## Video 5 — Upload plans and set the scale

**Goal:** plans uploaded and every sheet has the right scale, ready to measure.
**Start on:** a job's **Plans** tab. Have a PDF plan set ready in a folder.

| | DO | SAY |
|---|----|-----|
| 1 | Show the Plans tab. | "Every job's plans live on the Plans tab. Let's bring a set in." |
| 2 | Point at **Client sees them**. | "If your client should see these plans in their portal, tick this first. Otherwise only your team sees them." |
| 3 | Drag the PDF from the folder onto the drop box. | "Just drag your PDFs onto this box. You can drop several at once, up to 100 megabytes each." |
| 4 | Wait for it to finish; point at the sheet chips. | "Each page shows up as a sheet. 'No scale' in yellow means we still need to tell the program how big the drawing is." |
| 5 | Click **Rename pages**, type over a couple of names. | "Rename the pages so they're easy to find — First Floor, Second Floor, Roof. Enter moves to the next page." |
| 6 | Click **Done**, then **Measure**. | "Now click Measure to open the takeoff screen." |
| 7 | Tick **all sheets**, then open the scale box; point at **Recommended**. | "Up here is the scale. If every sheet uses the same scale, tick all sheets first. When the program can tell the scale, it marks it Recommended — it's only a suggestion, so check what the architect wrote on the sheet." |
| 8 | Pick the scale printed on the sheet (like 1/4" = 1'-0"). | "The sheet says quarter inch equals a foot, so I'll pick that. It's set right away." |
| 9 | Click **Calibrate**, click both ends of a long dimension, type **Feet** and **Inches**, click **Set scale**. | "If it's a scanned plan, or you're not sure it printed true to scale, use Calibrate. Click both ends of a long dimension — the longer the better — type the real length, and click Set scale." |
| 10 | Click **Ruler** and measure a printed dimension. | "Always check it with the Ruler. Click two points on a dimension printed on the plan — it reads the same, so we're right." |
| 11 | Open the sheet picker list. | "Any sheet still missing a scale says 'no scale' in this list." |
| 12 | — | "That's it. The plans are in and the scale is set. Next video, we start measuring." |

## Video 13 — Build the estimate

**Goal:** an estimate built from the takeoff, finished with the rest of the job's costs and saved.
**Start on:** a job that has a takeoff measured but no estimate yet (or the sample job).

| | DO | SAY |
|---|----|-----|
| 1 | Click the **Estimate** tab. | "Once the takeoff is measured, open the Estimate tab." |
| 2 | Pick a template (or **Blank estimate**), click **Create estimate**. | "Start from one of your templates, or a blank one. The takeoff comes in by itself — you don't retype anything." |
| 3 | Point at a line with the ruler icon; click the icon. | "Lines with this ruler came from the takeoff. Click it to see the measurements behind them. If you change the takeoff, these update." |
| 4 | Point at a Division row, a Category under it, and Items under that. | "The estimate is set up three levels deep. Divisions are the big groups. Categories are what your client sees — like Kitchen Cabinets. Items are your cost lines underneath, and the client never sees those." |
| 5 | Click **Add from cost codes**, tick a few, add them. | "To add more, click Add from cost codes and tick what you need. Or drag them in from the Cost codes list on the side." |
| 6 | Click **+ Add item** on a category; type a description, quantity and unit cost. | "Add a line under any category with Add item — a sub's price, a rental, whatever's left." |
| 7 | Click a category name; show **Specifications**. | "Click a category's name to open its details. What you type under Specifications is what the client reads on the proposal." |
| 8 | Click **Markup & tax**; set the profit by cost type. | "Markup and tax sets your profit. You can mark up material, labor and subs differently." |
| 9 | Point at the bottom bar: Est. cost, Est. profit, Est. total, Base price. | "Down here are the totals — your cost, your profit and the price. The base price is what goes on the proposal. Keep it matched to the estimate, or round it to your own number and it shows your profit at that price." |
| 10 | Click **Save changes** → **Save over v1**. | "Nothing saves until you click Save changes. Leaving the page without saving asks you first." |
| 11 | Click **View proposal**. | "When it's ready, click View proposal to see exactly what your client gets. That's the next video." |
